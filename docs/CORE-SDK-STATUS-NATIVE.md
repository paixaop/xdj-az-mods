# Native deck-status capture

The passive producer runs in azstem on the identified EP147 ELF profiles. It shares the constructed DjPlayerUnit and PlayerCore lifetime gate with the command implementation. With commands disabled it installs the owner and property observation hooks and advertises zero command capabilities. The core's `azmod.deck-status.off` setting disables passive startup. The private status descriptor has its own eventfd, bounded receipt storage and a single core-side consumer. The older observation interfaces retain their existing layouts.

For a complete, source-matched working loop, `AZ_STATUS_LOOP_MODE_TIME` identifies a sample-length loop. The native set-loop writer stores zero at property `+0xc8` and the OUT−IN sample span at `+0xd0`. `AZ_STATUS_LOOP_MODE_BEAT` identifies a beat-fraction loop. The beat writer stores one at `+0xc8` and the explicit fraction at `+0xd8`. Both set the loop property kind to 3 or 4. EXIT makes the working mode empty. A foreign source, incomplete interval or invalid fraction makes it stale. Unidentified active kinds and modes remain unsupported.

The panel's RELOOP handler reads retained kind zero and requires both native source positions. The 2.00 handler at `0x221fb50` calls the two-point predicate at `0x14a76b8`. Its 1.30 counterparts are `0x20b2568` and `0x13ec278`. A missing endpoint establishes `reloop_available=0`. With a complete source-matched loop, both retained range locks must be resident. The original checker reads the IN and OUT lock states at retained `+0x178` and `+0x200`. The 2.00 lock request, clear, residency commit and reset writers are `0x14a03c8`, `0x14a12e0`, `0x14a1698` and `0x14a1770`. Their 1.30 counterparts are `0x13e4f88`, `0x13e5ea0`, `0x13e6258` and `0x13e6330`. Both states equal to 2 means ready. EXIT leaves this eligibility intact when the source and locks survive. Control arbitration and command completion have their own outcomes.

If load invalidation revokes the captured source with no active replacement, the LOAD withdrawal covers the old loop, main cue and retained eligibility. The loop reader drops its cached association, and an already-running residency callback can leave without adding a gap for that old load. An unchanged lifecycle or a matched active replacement preserves scoped loss when a source check or changed writer fails.

## Position and reposition writers

The continuous block reader returns a source and sample position. EP147 commits the returned sample to Core `+0x28` only when its source ID matches the current position. The 2.00 store is at `0x14145dc` within `processContinuousBlock`. Its assertion at `0x1414428` names the compared member `sourceId`. The stock page-buffer specializations identify `pcmbuf::SourceId` as `trackid::TrackID`. The rate comes from Core `+0x68`, which its constructor initializes to 44100. A source-valid zero sample is usable.

A seek can adopt a new source before the matching facade completion. The bridge records that genuine `setCurrentPos` source change under its native fence and waits for matched completion. A paused owner frame then supplies its actual position if the twelve-byte source ID agrees with the accepted track. An owner that observes both the prior and the newly selected source under the same fence can supply the same baseline. Neither path reports transport or phrase traversal between the observations. A same-track reload with no witnessed adoption still waits for a positively identified writer.

In 2.00 the original JumpCommand calls the setter at `0x1567efc`, and the controller's direct branch calls it at `0x15696e4`. Owner overlap, unowned writes or unexplained source-head movement invalidate continuity. An old sample cannot repopulate the newer generation. Source coordinates, the load fence, writer order and motion generation are copied before reducing a head. A confirmed generic setter landing establishes a position baseline.

The native reducer holds an early measurement from a newer generation until the writer's preceding cut arrives. It applies the held measurements in writer order and assigns public field revisions in that order. A declared capture gap drops the held samples and permits a fresh baseline. If the bounded wait fills, recovery reports the potentially skipped position, phase, phrase and transport routes.

The Slip background writer brackets its sample with the same motion generation as the audible head. A SEEK, JUMP or WRAP withdraws an older Slip coordinate at the barrier. A sample from the new generation becomes eligible after that barrier commits. A private peer that omits the generation can still report background-clock absence, while its coordinates remain unavailable.

JUMP requires a stock callback owned by the current deck's CueContext or BeatJumpFunction, a qualified initial loop command, or the owned direct request described below, plus an actual matching setter commit. For example, `BeatJumpFunction::beatJumpOnQua` constructs the concrete BeatJumpCommand with its owner at `+0x20` and uses its own Quantize member at `+0x10` on both images. WRAP additionally requires the stock active-loop command/callback chain, exact working property, reversible intervals and a source-qualified departure equivalent to or beyond the crossed boundary in its actual observation window, and a landing equivalent to the opposite boundary in its committed-point window. When a repeated target equals the prior sample, the SDK verifies the setter's stock maximum-position branch before claiming the occurrence. The default max getters are `0x765ff0` and `0x7a78b8` respectively. A custom provider or an unidentified action can leave classification or endpoint evidence unavailable.

Initial loop activation can reposition inside the loop through a temporary quantized command interval. JumpController constructs its forward/reverse targets around the requested point in descending order; the one-time typed Loop callback has a nonzero owned Play callback. Both distinguish this setup from the recurring ascending loop interval and hidden callback without Play. This is JUMP: the actual committed point must match one of that command's own source endpoints at its source rate with `N=1`, and lie within the active loop. The command must own this Core and source holder, its typed Loop callback must own the current unit basis, working property and forward/reverse intervals, and a measured departure window must be available. This proof uses the native command target, not equality between the jump distance and loop length. Ordinary recurring boundary traversal remains WRAP with its measured departure window and committed-point landing window. The initial JUMP emits one phrase FROM/TO discontinuity without reconstructing intermediate crossings.

The owned Quantize relay can also commit a direct RELOOP position with no callback. Its two admitted call sites forward the native position unchanged to this unit’s JumpController. The producer checks the exact call site, controller, Quantize and play/pause owners, active loop source and load, and matching Core/loop sample rate. The requested point may be inside the loop; it is retained with its source clock and must be committed exactly with `N=1`. This is a JUMP, while later recurring traversal retains the separate WRAP rules.

Each motion receipt carries separate departure and landing windows, with sample counts and their actual clock rates. The continuous writer takes its count from the original `RawArrayRef<Float2>` and its rate from the owned Core Hertz field. `processWithReservedCommnadChain` uses that same Hertz field to convert reserved time into a render subblock. An explicit setter commits one integer source-sample point and carries a one-sample window. A missing private tail leaves the window unknown. All boundary and equality decisions use `compare_timestamps()`. Normal rate-adjusted playback can depart one source sample past OUT, which remains equivalent inside the measured render window. The receipt preserves that measured departure.

The initial native source adoption can be copied through several position setters before load completion, including a nonzero first beat. The loader first uses an owned BackCue callback, then controller-only copies; the callback must identify this Core’s cue and play/pause owners. Consecutive copies preserve the opening witness only while the same source request remains pending, both actual endpoints equal the adopted coordinate at its source rate, and no render or classified action intervenes. Load completion, changed source or rate, a moved point, or a broken setter sequence ends that proof. The reducer separately checks zero or the first phrase boundary; loading a saved cue in a later phrase does not create a START transition. The outer owner frame and its first matched continuous writer share one seed routine under their existing observation or head reservation. This covers load completion inside an already-running owner frame, and a later outer-frame visit cannot duplicate the seed.

Standalone Beat Jump can call the controller synchronously with a null callback. The grid-aware function starts at `0x14574b8` in 1.30 and `0x1512b80` in 2.00. It computes the destination from the source's beat grid and can delegate to `BeatJumpFunction::beatJumpNoQua` at `0x1455f30` or `0x1511618`. The producer verifies that the BeatJumpFunction owns the current deck's play/pause and quantize children, the original quantize call reaches this deck's controller, and the requested source ID and sample are actually committed. Both standalone buttons on the 1.30 emulator produced direct phrase transitions in the combined and phrase-only subscriptions at 1 Hz. Needle search uses its distinct nominal NeedleJumpedCommand, owner and play/pause child. An unclassified controller relocation on an active path reports transport and phrase history loss and retains the measured landing.

## Live cue collections

Hot A–H occupy eight independent CueContext pointer slots starting at `+0x20`. Each owned receipt copies all eight slots and their source revisions. A single native revision can change one slot without losing an intervening public value. Multiple unresolved revisions, a replaced container or simultaneous slot changes with unknown order require scoped recovery. Initialization of an already identical color preserves the public revision.

The hot property copies the automatic-loop byte at `+0xe1` and the `CueComment` shared owner at `+0xe8` under its revision at `+8`. The comment owns a GNU string, which the capture copies as bounded UTF-8 inside the player frame. The outer point writers at `0x13fcbc0` and `0x14b7ef8` clear the old comment after the nested point and color commits. Those two increments form one observed edit when the prior property edition and load still match. An older private audio library supplies the original cue values without the optional metadata tail.

If load invalidation revokes the old bank between the load read and source match, the LOAD withdrawal covers that bank while no replacement is active. A failed match on an unchanged lifecycle or with an active replacement still reports live-hot-cue loss.

The working-property copy takes the destination in X0 and the actual source property in X1. The 2.00 recall at `0x14ad4f0` selects a CueKind from the context and passes that exact property into `0x149f000`, which copies it to the working property. The copy writers are `0x13e7290` and `0x14a26d0`. When the source is an owned A–H loop, the receipt names its bank provider, bank edition and slot. Publication pins the live-hot collection revision and its full row. An edit or capture gap invalidates the link. Copies from foreign properties, including ones with identical bounds, cannot establish an association.

The memory producer binds the concrete PlayMusicPack carried by the same accepted native request and completion. The nominal pack, requested and populated TrackID, unit and load fences must agree. Its builder replaces the memory holder at `+0xe8`. The typed holder exchanges its immutable content at `+0x28` before notifying listeners. Capturing the broadcast preserves nested B→A edits even when the consumer wakes later. A bounded seed cache holds genuine native references acquired in the factory or setters. A holder or content missing from that cache remains loading until an owned writer supplies it. A worker decodes the immutable 128-byte rows and releases the token on delivery, rejection or recovery. Independent overlapping writers and unavailable order produce loss scoped to the memory collection. Native memory rows with inconsistent clocks or more than the supported capacity become stale.

Hot and memory collection publication retains the captured integer sample coordinates in owned reducer sidecars through queued delivery. A memory reader appends these coordinates in a size-gated private output tail; an older reader's public seconds are never converted back to integers. Mixed-rate rows convert exactly to a checked common integer clock. A uniform bank carries its actual source rate and one-sample comparison window; a mixed bank has no inferred shared observation window. Saved-loop references resolve their witnessed provider, load, collection revision and row identity, then copy the canonical row from that immutable collection at the publication cut. Bounds do not establish the association.

The live banks also carry the native repository's whole-millisecond keys, derived from the original cached microseconds. The repository can exchange its in-memory cue content before the storage request completes. Storage flags therefore require the committed FILE cue edition for the exact loaded reference. Hot rows match by slot. Memory rows require a unique typed storage key on both sides. `saved` indicates a persisted counterpart and `modified` indicates a known difference from that counterpart or a new unsaved row. A missing or ambiguous storage edition withdraws the flags while the native row remains readable. A storage flag change advances the public collection revision independently of the original native writer edition.

Both original repository-update functions return the owning holder through AAPCS64's indirect result register x8. The 2.00 function calls the synchronous broadcast at `0x1883770` after its own inlined exchange. The corresponding 1.30 call is at `0x17281a8`. A native memory point with an absent OUT microsecond sentinel can retain a zero in its OUT sample cache.

The stock forward and previous MEMORY CALL paths retain the exact immutable content while iterating it. At the four selected-row call sites, x22 is the zero-based row index and x4 is the row the firmware just copied from that index. The forward path retains its content reference at caller sp+0x78 and the previous path at sp+0x90. The C++ callback checks the matched PlayMusicPack, active holder, immutable content, deck, scope and bank edition before admitting the observation. The stock CueLoadFacade can apply the property synchronously or copy it into a queued task at +0x28. A queued association uses the task's incarnation, facade and sequence and is consumed once. A destroyed task cannot identify a later recall.

## Native duration and remaining time

| EP147 | Track length setter | Track reset | PageBuffer length read | Track destructor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.30 | `0x778660` | `0x773fc0` | `0x770eb8` | `0x7668a8` |
| 2.00 | `0x7b9f60` | `0x7b58c0` | `0x7b27b8` | `0x7a8128` |

`pcmbuf::pagebuf::Track::setTotalLength` commits `source_time::Sample` at row `+0x30`. The page-filled callback supplies this writer; the bridge copies its committed result, including the native limit, rather than its requested argument. The PageBuffer read returns that sample count unchanged through ReadableTimeStretchAdapter. Core's actual Hertz at `+0x68` supplies its clock. An unknown native sentinel or invalid Hertz withdraws duration. A valid result publishes load-scoped native `METADATA.duration_s` and one-source-sample `duration_precision_s`, retaining integer ticks for comparisons.

The PageBuffer constructor at `0x7779a8` / `0x7b92a8` passes its embedded config at parent `+8` into each Track. Track retains that reference at `+8`. Within a setter, reset or destructor frame, the bridge derives the live parent from this native reference and verifies the exact types, current vector bounds and `0xd8` row stride. The Unit lifetime gate protects its owned Basis and adapter, and the current mapped PCM address is compared with that parent without dereferencing a borrowed mapped provider. The row's TrackID must match both Core and the accepted load. The producer retains the owner-qualified buffer address only as an identity alongside its unit, load and source fence; it never dereferences that cached address.

Initial and recovery seeds copy the result inside the concrete PageBuffer read already invoked by the native UnitStateServer publication. The bridge adds no getter calls. Before and after that native publication it checks the current Unit-owned adapter identity, load fence and actual rate. Setter, reset and destructor interference rejects an older seed. Both PageBuffer destructor variants destroy their rows through the hooked non-deleting Track destructor before freeing the parent; this withdraws duration before source retirement. Unit retirement removes duration coverage after draining the lifetime gate.

The duration lane has independent capability and loss reporting. The consumer atomically replaces the two duration fields, preserves ready FILE fallback when native duration is unavailable, and recomputes remaining and eligible forward estimates through the existing reducer. A duration receipt does not create ENDED or a phrase transition. Duration loss withdraws its derived times while preserving the measured head. Every time comparison uses `compare_timestamps()` with the actual source clock and window.

Page-filled callbacks repeatedly commit the same total length. After checking the current load and exact TrackID, an unchanged row from the qualified buffer needs no new Unit reservation or receipt. An atomic, sequence-checked scalar certificate preserves that proof while a snapshot reader holds the duration reservation. Replaying an equal receipt for a seed or retry leaves the certificate sealed and readable. A changed or reset selected row still reports loss when admission is unavailable, including overlapping scalar publication and Unit reservations. A different buffer carrying the same track cannot declare loss for the selected reader. Reader seeds do not mutate native duration and retry a reservation collision without reporting a missed write. Their retry flag is independent of reported loss, so a deferred seed cannot suppress a later changed writer's gap.

ARM producer, reducer, combined native fixtures and ASan/UBSan variants passed in the 1.30 emulator. They cover owned reader seeds, exact one-sample changes, wrong row/source, invalid rates, reset/destruction, capture loss, stale seeds, load retirement, late remaining-time updates, estimate guards and FILE fallback. Static entry and layout evidence covers both ELF profiles. Integrated firmware runs on both profiles supplied native duration 178.76 seconds at 44,100 Hz before FILE metadata, retained native precedence afterward, and kept duration stable through playback and loop controls. The 2.00 run also reached the exact terminal coordinate with continuous callbacks. A correctly declared 48 kHz file on 1.30 independently exposed native length 7,883,316 at 44,100 Hz: file metadata does not determine the native coordinate clock. Other media-source cells remain unqualified.

## Signed transport speed

| EP147 | Core start / stop | Pitch speed setter | Scratch speed setter | Cascaded manager operate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.30 | `0x1359b40` / `0x1359bf8` | `0xda9950` | `0xdad478` | `0xd90000` |
| 2.00 | `0x1414eb0` / `0x1414f68` | `0xe343c8` | `0xe37ed0` | `0xe1ae10` |

Core's source-qualified active-render flag is at `+9`. A cleared flag silences its continuous output. For an active renderer, its ReadableTimeStretchAdapter supplies the CascadedTimeStretchManager at `+0x18`. The original operate call selects the effect at manager `+0x38` or `+0x40` through the mode task at `+0x18`. The producer accepts the two nominal effects and copies their signed applied speed at `+0x10`; direction and magnitude have separate availability. The speed task's own getter exposes a queued target at task `+0x48`, which can precede the audible result. Source adoption, a change of effect and an unavailable effect have separate outcomes. During acceleration or braking, the producer verifies the committed endpoint speed against the last actual source-advance sample. Earlier magnitudes may differ and zero samples are allowed within a single direction. A missing or corrupt advance buffer, a mismatched endpoint, or mixed nonzero directions withdraws the affected measurement. The 1.30 emulator delivered the measured ramp and final pause without a transport gap.

An active Core can temporarily clear unreadable output without generating new source advances. A scoped witness around the original continuous call observes the real common rate generator and checks the selected internal effect, source and load before and after. If that generator did not run, the producer retains the native scalar measurement and does not inspect an older sample vector. A generated block, external scratch vector, nested call, foreign generator or changed owner keeps the strict vector validation. Equal head positions alone never establish freshness.

## Play and cue audition

| EP147 | Play latch writer | Pause latch writer | Cue-play writer / reset | Normal play return | Main cue audition return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.30 | `0x1421298` | `0x1424f58` | `0x13e3bd8` / `0x13e3da8` | `0x13c32fc` | `0x1369cd8` |
| 2.00 | `0x14dca40` | `0x14e06e8` | `0x149eed0` / `0x149f0a0` | `0x147e514` | `0x1425028` |

PlayPauseFunction and CueContext belong to the same validated player and source. The normal-play handler can set the play latch while the previously set cue is still active, then clear the cue flag. The main cue audition takes a distinct caller through the same BeatSyncFunction virtual method. The 2.00 immediate branch at `0x1499bd8` tail-calls the genuine play setter and preserves that caller. On release the native pause writer clears the latch and the deck returns to its cue. The status reader uses those committed writes and records a gap when an unclassified control mode or unobserved change breaks coverage. The triplet from StateNotificationServer remains independently available.

In 2.00, `PlayPauseFunctionHandler::onStopped` at `0x146b7c8` asserts that its PlayController reports inactive and advances a pending operation from phase 1 to 2. The nominal PlayController getter at `0x155ed28` reads its Core's render-active byte at `+9`. The matching 1.30 getter is `0x14a3898`. This byte identifies when rendering has stopped. A source can remain loaded and paused as its vinyl brake finishes. The handler does not establish an additional user transport mode for that interval. A distinct `AZ_PLAYBACK_STOPPED` transition needs a source that separates these cases.
